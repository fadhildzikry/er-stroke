🏥 Post-tPA Monitoring Protocol
▼
  • ICU/monitored bed for minimum 24 hr
  • Neuro checks q15 min × 2 hr, q30 min × 6 hr, q1 hr × 16 hr
  • BP q15 min × 2 hr, q30 min × 6 hr, q1 hr × 16 hr; maintain <180/105
  • No antithrombotic agents × 24 hr
  • No arterial punctures at non-compressible sites × 24 hr
  • No urinary catheter × 30 min post-infusion
  • CT at 24 hr (or sooner if neurological deterioration)
  • If angioedema → stop tPA, diphenhydramine + ranitidine ± epinephrine
🚨 Symptomatic ICH Post-tPA
Sudden neurological worsening + new ICH on CT within 36 hr. Stop tPA (if still running). Call neurosurgery. Consider cryoprecipitate (Fibrinogen <150), FFP, platelet transfusion if thrombocytopenic. Tranexamic acid 1 g IV may be considered.
Hemorrhagic Transformation Types
HI-1: petechiae along infarct margin · HI-2: confluent petechiae · PH-1: hematoma ≤30% infarct · PH-2: hematoma >30% (symptomatic — sICH)
💊 Tenecteplase (TNK) — 2026: Class I
▼
🆕 AHA/ASA 2026 — Now Class I (upgraded from IIb)
Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg IV single bolus (max 25 mg) is now a Class I recommendation — equivalent to alteplase for AIS within 4.5 hr. Either agent is acceptable; tenecteplase preferred for simplicity and ease of transfer logistics.
Dose: 0.25 mg/kg IV single bolus (max 25 mg)
Advantage 2026: Single push, no 60-min infusion → faster transfer if bridging to EVT center ("drip and ship" friendlier)
Evidence: EXTEND-IA TNK, NOR-TEST, TRACE-2, AcT trials
Note: Practical adoption accelerating rapidly post-2026 guideline

🧠 Basilar Artery Occlusion (BAO) — 2026: Class I
▼
🆕 2026 — BAO EVT Now Class I (with criteria)
NIHSS ≥10 + mild ischemic damage + presentation within 24 hr → EVT Class I (ATTENTION, BAOCHE trials). Previously Class IIa.
⚠️ High Mortality — Treat Aggressively
BAO untreated = 80–95% mortality. Even patients in coma may benefit.
FeatureDetail

NIHSSUnreliable — underestimates posterior strokes

iv tPAGive if within window; don't delay EVT
Posterior Circulation — Red Flags
  • Crossed deficits (ipsilateral face, contralateral body)
  • Bilateral weakness / bilateral extensor plantar
  • Gaze palsy (horizontal = pontine, vertical = midbrain)
  • Locked-in syndrome: quadriplegia + anarthria + intact vertical gaze
  • Horner syndrome + ipsilateral ataxia + contralateral pain loss (Wallenberg)
  • Sudden coma without preceding TIA or prodrome

Blood Pressure Targets by Scenario
🚫 tPA candidate — Pre-treatment
< 185 / 110 mmHg
Achieve before tPA administration. Use labetalol or nicardipine. If not achievable → tPA contraindicated.
💉 Post-tPA (first 24 hr) — 2026 Update
< 180 / 105 mmHg
🆕 2026 Class III: Do NOT lower SBP to <140 mmHg post-tPA (mild-moderate stroke). Intensive BP lowering post-tPA has not improved outcomes (ENCHANTED trial) and may cause harm. Target <180/105 remains the ceiling.
🔬 Post-EVT (successful recanalization) — 2026 NEW
Do NOT target < 140 mmHg (first 72 hr)
🆕 2026 Class III: Against treating to SBP <140 mmHg for first 72 hr after successful recanalization (anterior circulation LVO). Supported by ENCHANTED2/MT and OPTIMAL-BP trials showing harm with aggressive lowering post-EVT.
🔬 No reperfusion therapy (ischemic)
Permissive to 220 / 120 mmHg
Do NOT treat unless BP >220/120, hypertensive emergency, or specific comorbidity (aortic dissection, ACS, HF). "Permissive hypertension" maintains penumbra perfusion.
📉 After 24 hr (stable ischemic)
< 140 / 90 mmHg
Start antihypertensive 24–48 hr after onset if no reperfusion complications and patient stable.
🩸 ICH — Acute (SBP 150–220)
Target SBP 130–150 mmHg
ATACH-2 / INTERACT2 data. SBP target 130–150 mmHg safe; aggressive lowering <120 may harm. Avoid SBP <130.
🩸 ICH — Acute (SBP >220)
Continuous infusion, reduce to 160 mmHg
Continuous nicardipine or labetalol infusion. Avoid bolus-only therapy for SBP >220.

🔬 Acute Stroke Workup
▼
Immediate (concurrent with triage)
  • CT Non-Contrast Head — hemorrhage exclusion
  • CT Angiography Head + Neck — LVO, dissection, stenosis
  • CT Perfusion (CTP) — if late window EVT considered
  • Blood glucose (finger-stick stat)
  • 12-lead ECG
Emergency Labs
  • CBC with differential
  • PT/INR, aPTT — anticoagulation status
  • BMP (Na, K, Cr, glucose)
  • Troponin (ACS co-morbid; also neurogenic cardiac injury)
  • Anti-Xa level / direct thrombin time if on DOAC
  • Type & Screen
Early Inpatient (within 24 hr)
  • MRI Brain with DWI/ADC/FLAIR/GRE/SWI
  • Echocardiogram (TTE ± TEE for cardioembolic workup)
  • Continuous telemetry × ≥24 hr (minimum), ideally 72 hr (AF detection)
  • Fasting lipid panel, HbA1c
  • Hypercoagulable panel (if age <50, no traditional RF): Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S, AT-III, anticardiolipin Ab, β2-glycoprotein
  • ESR/CRP (vasculitis?)
  • Carotid duplex / TCD (if CTA not done)
Selected / Specific Situations
  • LP if SAH suspected and CT negative (xanthochromia)
  • HbS if sickle cell suspected
  • ANA, ANCA, anti-dsDNA if vasculitis (young pt, multifocal, meningeal signs)
  • HIV, VDRL/RPR (endemic areas, risk factors)
  • Urine/serum drug screen (cocaine, amphetamines)
  • TEE if PFO/aortic arch thrombus suspected
  • Prolonged cardiac monitoring (30-day Holter / implantable loop recorder) for cryptogenic stroke

⚠️ Stroke Mimics — Critical to Recognize
10–30% of suspected strokes are mimics. Some mimics (hypoglycemia, Todd's) have urgent treatments. tPA in mimics = not harmful in most cases.

💉 Thrombolysis — 2026 Time Window Expansion
▼
Standard Window (0–4.5 hr) — Both agents Class I
Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg OR Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg — both Class I. No lower NIHSS threshold. No advanced imaging required. Give ASAP regardless of EVT plans.
Extended Window (4.5–9 hr) — NEW Class IIa
IV thrombolysis reasonable with advanced imaging showing salvageable brain tissue (MRI DWI/FLAIR mismatch OR CT perfusion mismatch). Based on EXTEND, ECASS-4, EPITHET. Covers wake-up stroke and unwitnessed onset with LKW >4.5 hr but <9 hr.
Very Late Window (9–24 hr, LVO, no EVT) — NEW Class IIb
May be considered for LVO patients with salvageable tissue who cannot undergo thrombectomy. Supported by HOPE, TRACE-III. Note: TIMELESS (tenecteplase) was negative. Still needs advanced imaging selection.
DAPT as Alternative for Minor Non-Disabling Stroke ≤4.5 hr
DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) may be an alternative to IV thrombolysis in minor non-disabling stroke within 4.5 hr (ARAMIS, PRISMS data). Does NOT replace tPA for disabling strokes.

▼
Post-tPA (mild-moderate stroke)
Class III: Do NOT lower SBP to <140
ENCHANTED trial: intensive post-tPA BP lowering did not improve functional outcomes. Maintain <180/105 as upper ceiling; that's enough.
Post-EVT successful recanalization (first 72 hr)
Class III: Do NOT target SBP <140
ENCHANTED2/MT + OPTIMAL-BP: aggressive lowering post-EVT causes harm in anterior circulation LVO. Permissive approach for 72 hr after recanalization.

Class III: Do NOT target 80–130 with IV insulin
No outcome benefit; significantly increases severe hypoglycemia. Target 140–180 mg/dL remains appropriate.

🔭 Remaining Knowledge Gaps (per 2026 Authors)
▼
  • Medium/distal vessel occlusion EVT — devices improving, trials ongoing
  • Advanced imaging for thrombolysis in extended windows in resource-limited settings
  • Neuroprotection — no benefit shown yet; may be revisited as adjunct to reperfusion
  • Pediatric stroke — major evidence gap throughout
  • Optimal anticoagulation timing post-AF stroke (1-3-6-12 rule still debated)
  • Thrombectomy in tandem occlusion (ICA + MCA) — growing evidence, not definitive
  • AI-assisted prehospital detection and workflow optimization
